1.1 — Construction input costs jump 13.3% in a year; copper wire & cable up 25.5% — Fri Nov 13, 2026

  • What happened: The Associated General Contractors of America (AGC) released its analysis of federal producer price index data on Fri Nov 13, 2026: the PPI for inputs to new nonresidential construction rose 13.3% from Oct 2025 to Oct 2026, the largest year-over-year increase in more than four years. Diesel fuel PPI more than doubled (+115.2%), steel mill products rose 32.9%, aluminum mill shapes 23.3%, copper and brass mill shapes 23.9%, and truck freight 24.6%. ENR's same-day report of the Associated Builders and Contractors (ABC) analysis adds that copper wire and cable rose 25.5% year-over-year, that all survey respondents expect further price increases for transformers and switchgear, and that AGC's index of prices contractors charge rose only 5.5% against the 13.3% input rise. Lead times for structural steel and mineral wool insulation have lengthened. AGC published a new edition of its Construction Inflation Alert with the release.
  • Why it matters: Input inflation is outrunning bid prices by more than 2-to-1, and it is concentrated in exactly what electricians buy — copper, steel, freight, and electrical gear (transformers/switchgear).
  • Sharma impact: Direct and immediate. Quote validity windows and material escalation language matter more than ever on builder work; buy copper and gear early on signed jobs where terms allow; expect switchgear/transformer lead-time and price pressure on larger jobs and data-center-adjacent work.
  • Source: AGC of America (primary analysis) — https://www.agc.org/news/2026/11/13/construction-input-costs-rise-133-october-2025-october-2026-price-increases-spread-fuel-metals-other ; ENR (trade press, ABC analysis + copper wire & cable figure) — https://www.enr.com/articles/64271-construction-prices-remain-flat-in-october-steel-and-copper-costs-surge

1.2 — Brooklyn building collapse kills eight; citywide inspection sweep and criminal probe — collapse Wed Nov 11; developments Thu Nov 12 – Sat Nov 14, 2026

  • What happened: On Wed Nov 11, 2026, a three-story building at 1321 Flatbush Ave, Brooklyn, collapsed into an adjacent excavation site (2625 Bedford Ave) while crews were digging next door; eight people were killed (including two children, ages 9 and 10) and 18 injured. The Associated Press reported Thu Nov 12 that rescue operations had ended and Mayor Zohran Mamdani ordered inspections at about 100 sites tied to companies involved in the project; later coverage (Nov 13–14) put the sweep at more than 160 sites, and the Brooklyn DA opened a criminal probe. City records show a stop-work order was issued in March 2026 over demolition without adequate protection for neighbouring buildings, work resumed in violation in April, and a Nov 2025 geotechnical report had flagged that the neighbouring building would need underpinning. Cause is not yet determined — investigators are examining excavation/shoring among other factors.
  • Why it matters: It is the week's starkest construction-safety and engineering story: excavation next to an occupied, older structure, a prior stop-work history, and an underpinning warning in the project record.
  • Sharma impact: Sharma doesn't excavate structures, but its TUG/underground crews dig next to existing buildings every week. The transferable rules: never treat a stop-work order or an engineer's protection note as paperwork, verify shoring/protection of adjacent structures before digging near them, and document site conditions — after an incident, the paper trail is the case.
  • Source: Associated Press via FireRescue1, Nov 13, 2026 — https://www.firerescue1.com/fire-safety-codes/nyc-orders-inspections-after-brooklyn-building-collapse-kills-8 ; follow-up records reporting, New York Post, Nov 13, 2026 — https://nypost.com/2026/11/13/us-news/nyc-okd-construction-near-collapse-despite-red-flags-records-sources/

1.3 — Data-center backlash: Climate Mayors releases a model local law to give cities control — Fri Nov 13, 2026

  • What happened: Construction Dive reported Fri Nov 13, 2026 that Climate Mayors has released a model local law for data-center development — a menu of approaches covering siting and permitting, operational standards, transparency (including prohibiting NDAs), community benefit agreements, and environmental mitigation. The release follows midterm-election results in which voters in dozens of localities across 10 states rejected data-center projects; a tracker cited in the article counts more than 500 local governments in 46 states that have placed moratoriums on data-center development.
  • Why it matters: Data centers are the one booming construction sector (see item 1.1), and the regulatory ground under them is shifting from "fast-track" to "prove it" — permitting risk is now a schedule risk on the industry's biggest jobs.
  • Sharma impact: Sharma's core is residential, but Florida data-center and light-industrial work is a growth lane for electrical subs. If Sharma bids such work, expect longer permitting, community-benefit conditions, and power/water scrutiny baked into schedules — and moratorium risk in some jurisdictions.

1.4 — FLORIDA: Builders say ICE surge is emptying First Coast jobsites; new-home costs up ~18% — Thu Nov 12, 2026

  • What happened: The First Coast Ledger (summarising a Jacksonville Daily Record report) reported Thu Nov 12, 2026 that Northeast Florida builders say stepped-up ICE enforcement is driving off workers — including, they say, legal workers — at master-planned communities such as SilverLeaf, Seven Pines and Wildlight. NEFBA president Scott Brannock estimated total cost to homebuyers is up about 18% since March, with knock-on costs such as roof trusses sitting crew-less for weeks adding $8,000–$12,000 per house; a framing contractor cited framing on a 2,000 sq ft house up $14,000. The article cites Deportation Data Project figures showing Jacksonville ICE detentions rising from 40 in Jan 2025 to 377 in Jul 2026.
  • Why it matters: Workforce availability — not demand — is the binding constraint on Florida residential construction right now, and it is repricing every trade's labour.
  • Sharma impact: Sharma hires and crews in the same Florida labour pool. Expect continued wage pressure, schedule slippage on builder jobs when other trades' crews thin out, and more builders asking subs to lock schedules earlier. (Figures are builder-side estimates reported by the source, not audited data — treat the 18% as an industry claim, not a statistic.)
  • Source: First Coast Ledger, Nov 12, 2026 (reporting the Jacksonville Daily Record) — https://www.firstcoastledger.com/builders-say-the-ice-surge-is-emptying-first-coast-jobsites/

1.6 — Just outside the window (labelled, not back-dated): Trimble launches Connect AI + AI scheduling at Dimensions — announced Mon Nov 9, 2026

  • What happened: At its Dimensions conference in Las Vegas (Nov 9–11), Trimble announced a new AI layer for Trimble Connect on Mon Nov 9, 2026: six capabilities available immediately (Automated Structural Modeling, AI Indoor Classification, 360 Reality Capture, AI Punch Walk, AI Procurement & Invoice Matching, and Trimble Connect Scheduling), plus Trimble Assist (conversational interface) and Agent Studio (build-your-own agents). Connect Scheduling links a master CPM schedule to live field data — look-aheads, RFIs, weather, submittals — and uses an agentic planning engine to flag risk and recommend schedule adjustments for human approval; it is available in North America to GCs and subs of all sizes. Trade coverage ran through the week (GPS World Nov 10; BIM Manager Academy's daily brief discussed it again Nov 13).
  • Why it matters: This is the clearest sign yet of where construction software is going: agents that draft and recommend, people who approve — including punch walks and invoice matching, two tasks subs actually spend office hours on.
  • Sharma impact: Not a buy-today item for a shop Sharma's size, but the direction matters: GCs running agentic scheduling will push more real-time schedule data demands onto subs, and AI invoice-matching on the GC side means Sharma's invoices/POs/delivery records need to match cleanly the first time.
  • Source: Trimble (primary release), Nov 9, 2026 — https://news.trimble.com/2026-11-09-Trimble-Launches-New-Trimble-Connect-Platform-AI-Capabilities-Aimed-at-De-Risking-Construction and https://news.trimble.com/2026-11-09-Trimble-Launches-New-AI-Accelerated-Scheduling-Solution-to-Help-Contractors-Protect-Project-Milestones
