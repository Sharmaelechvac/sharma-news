1.3 — Data-center backlash: Climate Mayors releases a model local law to give cities control — Fri Nov 13, 2026

Why it matters to Sharma: Sharma's core is residential, but Florida data-center and light-industrial work is a growth lane for electrical subs. If Sharma bids such work, expect longer permitting, community-benefit conditions, and power/water scrutiny baked into schedules — and…

  • What happened: Construction Dive reported Fri Nov 13, 2026 that Climate Mayors has released a model local law for data-center development — a menu of approaches covering siting and permitting, operational standards, transparency (including prohibiting NDAs), community benefit agreements, and environmental mitigation. The release follows midterm-election results in which voters in dozens of localities across 10 states rejected data-center projects; a tracker cited in the article counts more than 500 local governments in 46 states that have placed moratoriums on data-center development.
  • Why it matters: Data centers are the one booming construction sector (see item 1.1), and the regulatory ground under them is shifting from "fast-track" to "prove it" — permitting risk is now a schedule risk on the industry's biggest jobs.
  • Sharma impact: Sharma's core is residential, but Florida data-center and light-industrial work is a growth lane for electrical subs. If Sharma bids such work, expect longer permitting, community-benefit conditions, and power/water scrutiny baked into schedules — and moratorium risk in some jurisdictions.
