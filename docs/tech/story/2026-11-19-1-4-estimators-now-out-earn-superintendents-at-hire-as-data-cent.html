  • What happened: Construction recruiter Amundson Group published its Q3 2026 Construction Labor Market Report on Wed Nov 18, 2026 (Houston, via PR Newswire), drawn from the firm's own placements. Its headline finding: estimators now out-earn superintendents at the moment of hire — a median base salary of $117,500 for estimators, up 15% in a year, against $110,000 for superintendents; project managers lead at $125,000 and foremen stand at $88,400. Estimators and foremen led the firm's third-quarter placements, ahead of project managers, superintendents and executives. The report sets those figures against federal data: 298,000 construction jobs were open at the end of September, construction unemployment was 3.6% in October (matching the lowest October rate in 27 years of records, per BLS), and private spending on data-center construction rose 90% in the year to September (per Census). Labelled honestly: the salary figures are one recruiting firm's placement data, not a government survey — the federal figures it cites are the checkable backdrop.
  • Why it matters: Estimating capacity is the bottleneck the market is paying a premium for, because data-center programmes are bidding for the same estimators, superintendents and project managers as everyone else. When estimator pay rises 15% in a year, bid throughput — how many jobs a contractor can price well — becomes the scarce resource.
  • Sharma impact: A costing signal for Sharma's own bid operation: the market value of good estimating is rising fast, which raises the payoff from Sharma's takeoff-and-estimate discipline (and the cost of losing it). It also helps explain GC-side pricing behaviour on data-center-adjacent work. No action implied.
