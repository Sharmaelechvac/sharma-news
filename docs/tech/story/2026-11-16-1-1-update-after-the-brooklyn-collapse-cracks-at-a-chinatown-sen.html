  • What happened: The New York Post reported Sun Nov 15, 2026 that City Councilman Christopher Marte wrote to the NYC Department of Buildings after personally seeing "newly formed cracks" at Chung Pak, a 13-storey, 88-unit senior-housing complex at 96 Baxter St in Chinatown, directly adjacent to the secant-wall construction for the city's new jail at 125 White St. Marte asked the DOB to issue an immediate stop-work order until the impacts can be assessed and monitoring installed, saying no vibration or crack monitors have been in place since the demolition contractor left the site. Cracking complaints at Chung Pak date back to at least 2024, and residents sued last month over the project's around-the-clock schedule; the DOB did not respond to the Post's request for comment on Sunday. The renewed alarm follows the Wed Nov 11 Flatbush collapse (Edition 1, item 1.2), in which eight people were killed when a building fell into an adjacent excavation.
  • Why it matters: Two adjacent-construction cases in one week in the same city — one fatal, one caught at the cracking stage — show how fast "damage to the building next door" becomes a stop-work, legal and reputational event for the contractor doing the digging and building.
  • Sharma impact: Sharma's TUG and underground crews work next to occupied buildings constantly. The transferable discipline: photograph and document neighbouring structures before work starts, treat any reported cracking as a stop-and-check event, and keep monitoring/protection notes on file — the paper trail is what separates a scare from a shutdown.
