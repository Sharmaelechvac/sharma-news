Item 3A — Copper gives back the bounce and more: COMEX settles −4.70% at $5.3625/lb; LME three-month −3.5% in official trading at $12,054/t — Tuesday, November 24 - Source / links: Morningstar (Dow Jones Data Talk), Comex Copper Settles 4.70% Lower at $5.3625 — Data Talk, Nov 24, 2026 — https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202611246257/comex-copper-settles-470-lower-at-53625-data-talk ; Mining.com (with Reuters and Bloomberg), Copper price erases Nvidia rebound as investors steer clear of metals, Nov 24, 2026 — https://www.mining.com/copper-price-erases-nvidia-rebound-as-investors-steer-clear-of-metals/ ; ING, The Commodities Feed: Copper gives back Nvidia bounce, Nov 24, 2026 — https://think.ing.com/downloads/pdf/article/the-commodities-feed-nvidia-led-rally-lifts-gold-and-copper - Published: Tuesday, November 24, 2026 (inside the window). - Key figures: COMEX front-month copper for November delivery settled at $5.3625/lb, down 26.45 cents (−4.70%) on Tuesday (Dow Jones Market Data via Morningstar) — below Friday's $5.4770 settlement and the lowest front-month settlement in the current slide. The December contract traded at $5.437/lb in the morning (−3.5%) against its Monday settlement of $5.634. Tuesday's drop leaves COMEX copper 21% below its record settlement of $6.889/lb on September 9 — a bear market — down 16% in November and 3% on the year. LME three-month copper was down 3.5% at $12,054/t in official trading, after briefly trading below $12,000 (Reuters via Mining.com); late-session prints ran lower still (see Could not verify). ING's read: copper "gave back Monday's Nvidia-driven gains on Tuesday, falling more than 2% towards $12,000/t as focus shifted back to Chinese demand and US interest rates." The physical market did not loosen with the paper market: the Yangshan import premium climbed from under $100/t to around $500/t in a month, visible exchange inventories cover about two days of consumption, and BMO Capital Markets reported traders have booked an estimated 30,000–100,000 tonnes of U.S. cathode for direct shipment to China — metal leaving the U.S. because, in BMO's telling, traders are losing confidence that Washington will impose the threatened duties on refined copper (Chinese industry expects the duties to be dropped; Western participants still lean toward imposition). BMO sees the selloff extending until Washington signals its tariff intentions or prices fall toward $10,000–$11,000/t; Trafigura's cost curve shows about 10% of SX-EW production already loss-making near $13,000/t. The International Copper Study Group's latest bulletin: global mine production −1% year-over-year in September (Chile −15%, Peru −9%), refined demand −2%, a monthly deficit inside a year-to-date surplus. - Why it matters to Sharma: Monday's +2.74% COMEX bounce lasted one session; the two-way market warning in yesterday's edition is now the story. For Sharma's material conversations the levels are what count: the front month has fallen from $6.889 in September to $5.3625 — a 22% decline in the metal inside every wire and cable quote — while the physical premium in China sits near record levels and U.S. cathode is being shipped out on a tariff bet. That is a market where distributor quotes can move in either direction on a headline, and where the tariff decision, when it comes, reprices U.S. copper specifically. Short quote-validity windows in both directions remain the discipline; nothing Tuesday justifies locking a long price either way. Note also the direction of the BMO flow item for the tariff file: if duties are dropped, the U.S. premium structure that has supported COMEX pricing deflates further.
