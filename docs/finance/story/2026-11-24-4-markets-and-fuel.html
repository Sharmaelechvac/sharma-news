Bitcoin traded near $61,300 after plunging below $58,000 on Friday. Unresolved, and stated plainly by every source: Huang is scheduled to testify before Congress in early December on the espionage allegations, the circumstances of his resignation have not gone away, and Reuters' own market wrap asks how long the confidence lasts — Monday's rally removed the leadership uncertainty, not the underlying questions. - Why it matters to Sharma: read Monday for what it is — the removal of a single-company overhang that had dragged the whole AI buildout trade, and with it builder and supplier sentiment, into correction. It is not a change in Sharma's operating environment: the 10-year Treasury did not move (Section 1), mortgage money did not cheapen (Item 2A), builders rose only about 1.3% (Item 7A), and oil fell (Item 4B). The channels that actually reach Sharma — rates, fuel, copper, builder order books — are in the items around this one. The one genuine Sharma-relevant effect is second-order and worth naming: the AI data-centre buildout whose financing doubts drove last week's rout is the same buildout competing for electricians, switchgear and copper nationally; its central company's stabilisation keeps that demand — and that competition — alive into 2027.

WTI for January delivery settled at $80.17/bbl, down $3.24 (−3.88%) — its lowest since August 7 (WSJ). The morning Reuters read had Brent down only 0.65% at $91.97 (0844 GMT); the selling built through the U.S. session as supply news accumulated. The drivers, in the sources' order: Libya's Sharara pipeline restarted, with crude moving into storage at the Akakus facility in Zawiya, after an "unlawful group" had closed a valve — the National Oil Corporation's chairman confirmed the restart to Reuters on Monday; Gulf exports are recovering and Middle East barrels are reaching the Gulf of Oman despite Iran's tanker designations, which tanker tracking says have not reduced volumes; cautious optimism about U.S.–Iran diplomacy, including talks between Vice President Vance and Qatar's prime minister (Bloomberg, via Vantage); and the standing surplus — the IEA's expected Q4 oversupply and building U.S. stockpiles. Against all of that, the weekend's shipping attacks and Monday's mine claim (Item 4C) "limited the decline but did not stop it." Bank of America, in a Monday note, put 2027 Brent at an $80 average on geopolitics and low inventories (OilPrice). The next OPEC+ quota decision is due November 29. - Why it matters to Sharma: the surplus story has now beaten the war story for a fourth straight session, and Monday's margin was the largest yet — nearly 4% on WTI on a day when Iran claimed three more tankers burned. That is the market stating, with money, that physical barrels are moving. For Sharma the consequence is the pump trend in Item 4D: diesel's eighth straight day under $6 is now backed by a crude tape at three-month lows, which supports cautiously using the trend in near-term fuel and delivery assumptions. The discipline from prior editions still applies — the claim in Item 4C is unverified, and a confirmed cargo interruption remains the one event that reverses this in a day — but as of Monday's close the burden of proof has shifted to the disruption story.

Iran's IRNA added that the tankers sustained "powerful explosions" and caught fire despite being escorted by the U.S. Navy, with one vessel near the southern coast and two in the eastern part of the strait (Fars, via Trends MENA). U.S. Central Command had not responded to the claim, and the reports could not be independently verified (Jerusalem Post, via The Business Standard) — no UKMTO incident report confirming three mine strikes on Monday was located by the cutoff. The claim is therefore carried as UNVERIFIED and quarantined in Could not verify; it is not a confirmed interruption of any cargo. Separately and on the record: the Wall Street Journal reported the United States is investigating whether Iran was involved in cyberattacks on as many as 50 energy tankers, citing U.S. officials (via Reuters). The confirmed incident base continues to grow: UKMTO/JMIC counts show at least 22 attacks reported in November by Sunday — the second-highest monthly figure of the war — after October exceeded 30 for the first time; outbound transits were 115 in the seven days to November 18, down from 139 the week before (QNC / JMIC). The weekend also brought a Houthi claim, reported by Reuters on Monday, of an attack on a Saudi Arabian oil refinery on Sunday. No confirmed sailing of the 14+ tankers staged at Kharg Island (prior edition) was reported on Monday. - Why it matters to Sharma: Monday's oil tape (Item 4B) is the market's verdict on claims like this one: unconfirmed, it moves nothing. The Sharma-relevant distinction is between the claim and the confirmation — the day a mine strike on an escorted tanker is confirmed by UKMTO or CENTCOM, with a cargo lost, is the day the surplus pricing that has carried diesel down for eight straight days is repriced. Until then the operational posture is unchanged: use the fuel trend, keep validity windows honest, and treat every Hormuz headline as a claim to be verified, not a cost to be priced. This briefing will report the confirmation — or the debunking — when a primary source carries it.
