Item 2B — NAHB builder sentiment crashes to a 14-year low: HMI 25 in November (consensus 29); South at 27 — NAHB, Tuesday, November 17 - Source / links: NAHB press release, Nov 17, 2026 — https://www.nahb.org/news-and-economics/press-releases/2026/11/builder-sentiment-continues-to-fall-on-affordability-concerns — verified live ; Reuters, Nov 17, 2026 — https://dukecountry.fm/2026/11/17/us-homebuilder-sentiment-tumbles-to-more-than-14-1-2-year-low-in-november/ - Published: Tuesday, November 17, 2026, 10:00 AM ET (inside the window). - Key figures: The NAHB/Wells Fargo Housing Market Index fell 4 points to 25 in November, from 29 in October — the lowest since April 2012 and well below the 29 economists polled by Reuters expected. All three components fell: current sales conditions 28 (−6), sales expectations for the next six months 30 (−2), and prospective-buyer traffic 16 (−2) — its lowest in 15 years, excluding the start of the pandemic in 2020. Regional three-month moving averages fell everywhere: Northeast 34 (−4), Midwest 35 (−5), South 27 (−3), West 23 (−3). 46% of builders reduced prices in November — the highest share in the post-Covid period — with the average reduction at 6% for an eighth consecutive month; 68% offered sales incentives, also a post-Covid high and the 20th consecutive month at 60% or above. NAHB Chairman Bill Owens: "Stepped-up immigration enforcement is keeping legal workers off job sites, deepening labor shortages and driving up costs. Higher fuel prices are prompting subcontractors to raise their rates sharply, and with fewer buyers in the market, more builders are scaling back construction." Chief Economist Robert Dietz: "Mortgage rates above 7%, softer consumer confidence and an uncertain economic outlook are holding buyers back," citing material costs pushed higher by tariffs, the construction-worker shortage, and regulation. Reuters adds the 30-year fixed averaged 7.40% in mid-October, a three-year high, and has risen 120 bp since the U.S. and Israel attacked Iran in February. - Why it matters to Sharma: this is the single most important number of the week for Sharma — the builders Sharma works for are openly cutting prices and scaling back construction, and the NAHB chair names subcontractor rate increases as part of the cost squeeze. Expect harder price pressure on bids and slower starts into early 2027; the South at 27 is Sharma's own region.

September was unrevised at +0.8% ($746.6 billion). The 90% confidence interval for the monthly change includes zero, so Census says there is insufficient statistical evidence the change differed from zero. Ex-autos +0.6% (consensus +0.4%; September revised +1.1%); ex-autos and gasoline +0.5%. By category: miscellaneous store retailers +4.7%, gasoline stations +1.5%, health and personal care +1.5%, nonstore (online) +1.2%, building material and garden equipment and supplies dealers +0.5%, food services and drinking places +0.2%, general merchandise +0.1%; electronics and appliance stores flat; furniture and home furnishings −2.3%, motor vehicle and parts dealers −1.6%, clothing −0.7%, sporting goods/hobby/books −0.7%, food and beverage stores −0.2%. The figures are not adjusted for price changes, and CPI rose 0.4% in October — so inflation-adjusted sales fell. - Why it matters to Sharma: the consumer is still spending in nominal dollars, but the mix is unfriendly to Sharma's clients — furniture down 2.3% is a home-furnishing proxy for move-in demand, while the +0.5% at building-material dealers is the only bright line for the trades. A real (inflation-adjusted) decline in October sales undercuts the "consumer is fine" case for a housing rebound.
