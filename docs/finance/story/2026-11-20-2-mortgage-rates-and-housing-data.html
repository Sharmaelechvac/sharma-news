Item 2A — Freddie Mac weekly survey jumps 17 bp to 7.28%, ending four straight weekly declines — Thursday, November 19, noon ET - Source / links: Freddie Mac, Primary Mortgage Market Survey, Nov 19, 2026 — https://www.freddiemac.com/media-room ; Realtor.com, Mortgage Rates Surge Again as Stubborn Inflation Ends Brief Reprieve, Nov 19, 2026 — https://www.realtor.com/news/trends/mortgage-rates-this-week-november-19-2026/ - Published: Thursday, November 19, 2026, noon ET (inside the window). - Key figures: The 30-year fixed-rate mortgage averaged 7.28% for the week ending November 19, up 17 bp from 7.11% the prior week — the first increase after four consecutive weekly declines (7.40% → 7.32% → 7.24% → 7.18% → 7.11% → 7.28%). The 15-year fixed averaged 6.66%, up 21 bp from 6.45%. A year ago the 30-year averaged 6.26% and the 15-year 5.54% — the 30-year is now 102 bp above a year ago. Freddie Mac Chief Economist Sam Khater's comment was about shopping for rate quotes, not direction. Realtor.com's Anthony Smith: "The debate has shifted from whether the Fed hikes in December to how aggressively it moves. That is a tough backdrop for mortgage rates heading into what is already a seasonally slow stretch for the housing market." The same Realtor.com weekly update put the median listing price down 2.4% year-over-year to $405,000 and inventory up 8.6% year-over-year. The next survey is due Wednesday, November 25 (a day early, Thanksgiving week). - Why it matters to Sharma: the weekly headline builders quote to buyers just reversed — 7.28%, not 7.11%, is the number in this weekend's sales offices. Four weeks of easing are gone in one print, which keeps qualification tight heading into the seasonal slowdown and supports the NAHB picture (HMI 25, 46% cutting prices) from Tuesday's edition.

Item 2B — Daily mortgage rate edges down 1 bp to 7.45% on Thursday (Mortgage News Daily) - Source / links: Mortgage News Daily index via HousingNewsletters (MBS Live), rates as of Nov 19, 2026 — https://housingnewsletters.com/edieclark/Download/article?articleId=583ed46a36cb281fc853baab ; Real Estate News, A 'tough backdrop' for mortgage rates as holiday slowdown begins, Nov 19, 2026 — https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/11/19/a-tough-backdrop-for-mortgage-rates-as-holiday-slowdown-begins - Key figures: MND daily index, Thursday, November 19: 30-year fixed 7.45% (−1 bp from Wednesday's 7.46%), 15-year 6.99% (+1 bp), FHA 30-year 7.05% (+1 bp), jumbo 7.50% (unchanged). Real Estate News independently carried MND's Thursday 30-year at 7.45%. The daily index sits 17 bp above Freddie Mac's new weekly average (7.28%) — the normal gap between a same-day top-tier quote and a trailing application-week average. - Why it matters to Sharma: the daily money a buyer actually locks today is ~7.45%, not the 7.28% weekly headline. Both series rose on the week; neither supports expecting cheaper mortgage money before the December FOMC.

Item 2C — Initial jobless claims edge up to 203,000; continuing claims jump 70,000 to 1.763 million — DOL, Thursday, November 19, 8:30 AM ET - Source / links: Reuters, US weekly jobless claims edge up, but labor market remains resilient, Nov 19, 2026 — https://935thelloyd.com/2026/11/19/us-weekly-jobless-claims-increase-slightly-3/ ; PYMNTS, Jobless Claims Inch Up by 1,000 While Labor Market Remains Stable, Nov 19, 2026 — https://www.pymnts.com/economy/2026/jobless-claims-inch-up-1000-while-labor-market-remains-stable/ - Published: Thursday, November 19, 2026, 8:30 AM ET (inside the window). - Key figures: Initial claims rose 1,000 to a seasonally adjusted 203,000 for the week ended November 14 (prior week revised up to 202,000 from 200,000), against a Reuters-polled consensus of 197,000 — the second straight week above 200,000, after claims had hovered at or below 200,000 since mid-September. The four-week moving average rose 2,000 to 197,750. Continuing claims (week ended November 7) rose 70,000 to 1,763,000 — above the 1,710,000 consensus — with the insured unemployment rate unchanged at 1.1%; unadjusted continuing claims in Michigan rose 39,343, accounting for most of the national increase, and unadjusted initial filings in Michigan surged 6,095. The claims week covers the survey period for November's payroll report. The next release is Wednesday, November 25 (Thanksgiving week). - Why it matters to Sharma: layoffs remain low — this is a low-hire, low-fire labor market, not a cracking one — but the 70,000 jump in continuing claims says laid-off workers are taking longer to land, the same hiring freeze Sharma sees in builder back offices. No direct cost impact; it mildly undercuts the case that the Fed must hike 50 bp in December.

Item 2D — Philadelphia Fed index plunges to 6.3 in November (from 20.9); new orders negative; prices paid jump to 58.2 — Thursday, November 19, 8:30 AM ET - Source / link: Federal Reserve Bank of Philadelphia, Manufacturing Business Outlook Survey, November 2026, via RTTNews/dpa-AFX, Nov 19, 2026 — https://www.tradingview.com/news/dpa_afx:6531f92879c2b:0-philly-fed-index-plunges-to-six-month-low-in-november/ ; detail via QNC News, Nov 19, 2026 — https://qncnews.com/2026/11/19/philadelphia-fed-factory-index-slumps-to-6-3-as-new-orders-turn-negative-in-november/ - Published: Thursday, November 19, 2026 (inside the window). - Key figures: The diffusion index for current general activity fell to 6.3 in November from 20.9 in October — a six-month low, far below the ~20–23 consensus. The new orders index fell to −0.4 from +4.3, its lowest since May and a fourth straight decline; shipments rose to 25.7 from 15.3 (firms working through backlogs); the employment index rose to 11.6 from 9.1. On prices: the prices-paid index jumped to 58.2 from 43.2 — its highest since April — and prices received rose 7 points to 47.0, its highest since June 2022. The future general activity index edged up to 33.6 from 33.0. The survey covers the Philadelphia Fed district (Delaware, eastern Pennsylvania, southern New Jersey) — a regional factory read, not a national or Florida figure. - Why it matters to Sharma: the combination is the stagflation mix in miniature — demand (new orders) going negative while factories' input prices hit a seven-month high and their selling prices a four-year high. It corroborates what Sharma sees at the supply counter: end demand softening at the same time as input costs stay sticky, which is exactly the margin squeeze to defend against in bid validity and escalation language.

No new housing-starts, permits, or existing-home sales release fell in the window. The next starts/permits report is December 17; NAR's November existing-home sales are due December 9.
