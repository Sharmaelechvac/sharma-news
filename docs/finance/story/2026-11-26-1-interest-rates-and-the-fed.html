Item 1A — UPDATE: October PCE accelerates — headline 3.8% y/y, core 3.3% y/y (three-year high) — Wednesday, November 25 - Source / links: U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis, Personal Income and Outlays, October 2026 (released Nov 25, 2026) — https://www.bea.gov/taxonomy/term/716 ; Reuters, US inflation rises further in October, Nov 25, 2026 — https://www.reuters.com/business/us-inflation-rises-further-october-2026-11-25/ ; Marketplace, Core PCE inflation hit a 3-year high last month, Nov 25, 2026 — https://www.marketplace.org/story/2026/11/25/core-pce-inflation-hit-a-3year-high-last-month - Published: Wednesday, November 25, 2026 (inside the window). This is the print Tuesday's edition flagged as the week's test; it is an update to that watch item, not a repeat. - Key figures: Headline PCE +0.3% m/m (September +0.5%) and +3.8% y/y, in line with consensus and the highest since May 2023 on the wraps; September y/y was revised up to 3.7% (from 3.6% as first reported). Core PCE +0.3% m/m (same as September) and +3.3% y/y, above the 3.2% consensus and up from 3.1% in September — the highest core reading in nearly three years. Barron's (via QUE) put the three-month annualised core rate at 3.6%, up from an upwardly revised 3.0% in September. Personal income +$201.8B (+0.7%), disposable income +0.8%, nominal PCE spending +$63.8B (+0.3%) (goods +$11.8B, services +$52.1B), but real PCE fell $5.8B (less than −0.1%). Personal saving was $1.00 trillion, a 4.1% saving rate. The next PCE release is December 23 — after the December FOMC. - Why it matters to Sharma: The Fed's own preferred gauge is moving away from the 2% target on every horizon that matters — headline, core, and the three-month run rate. That locks in the December hike pricing in Item 1C and keeps upward pressure on the whole curve Sharma's builders' buyers borrow against. Real spending slipping while nominal spending rises is the stagflation-flavoured mix that squeezes entry-level buyers first.

The 10-year is 22 bp below a week ago (5.07%) and at roughly a two-month low on market quotes (Investopedia put the note at 4.87% late, down from 4.93% Tuesday's market close — a market quote, not the par print). The rally came from the morning equity plunge (a flight to quality), not from any softening in the inflation data, which landed hot at 8:30 AM ET the same morning. - Why it matters to Sharma: The 10-year at 4.85% is what pulled daily mortgage sheets to two-week lows (Section 2) even on a hot-PCE day. Treat it as volatility relief, not a trend break: the curve fell because stocks fell 3–5% intraday, and the December hike is still priced as near-done.

Item 1D — Beige Book: construction declined nationally; Sixth District residential fell moderately — Wednesday, November 25 - Source / links: Federal Reserve, Beige Book — National Summary, November 25, 2026 (prepared by the Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta; information collected on or before November 16, 2026) — https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/beigebook202611-summary.htm ; Morningstar (Dow Jones), Fed Contacts Expect Costs and Prices to Keep Rising — Beige Book, Nov 25, 2026 — https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202611256746/fed-contacts-expect-costs-and-prices-to-keep-rising-beige-book - Published: Wednesday, November 25, 2026, 2:00 PM ET (inside the window). - Key figures: Eight of 12 Districts grew slightly to moderately, four reported no change, and none declined — a fourth straight report with no declining District. Consumer spending was little changed (price-sensitive, selective). Residential real estate weakened and construction activity declined amid higher borrowing costs and elevated construction costs; manufacturing grew modestly on industrial/data-centre strength against housing-related weakness. Prices rose moderately; input cost growth outpaced selling-price increases, with energy, transportation, healthcare, insurance and tariff-related expenses plus raw materials, metals, freight and specialised imports all cited; contacts expected costs and prices to keep rising. Employment rose slightly (mostly attrition replacement); hiring difficulties persisted for skilled and technical roles; wages and benefits rose moderately, an uptick from the prior report. Banking was little changed with subdued loan demand. Atlanta (Sixth District, including Florida): activity grew modestly; employment stable; wages, costs and prices rose moderately; consumer spending steady; residential real estate declined moderately; commercial real estate, lending, manufacturing and transportation rose modestly; energy grew moderately. - Why it matters to Sharma: This is the Fed's own field report saying, in the same week as the data deluge, that (a) construction is contracting on borrowing + build costs, (b) firms cannot fully pass input costs through — the margin squeeze Sharma feels on fixed-price builder work — and (c) skilled/technical labour is still the hard hire, with wage growth re-accelerating to "moderate." The Sixth District line is the Florida read: housing down moderately, everything else modestly up.
