It is a fifth straight Brent gain on that series ($90.63 → $96.57 over five sessions, +6.6%). WTI had no official settlement Thursday (U.S. holiday); futures traded near $84.84 (+1.39% vs Wednesday's $83.68 close) on the aggregator, and ING's Thursday note put Brent above $95 and WTI above $85 intraday, a third consecutive session of gains, citing escalating Middle East tensions threatening regional flows. On the ground (CNN, Thursday): a shipping source said oil loadings at Ras Tanura were suspended after the Houthi-claimed attack a day earlier — it was unclear whether loadings have resumed, Aramco had not commented; satellite imagery showed a large plume of black smoke over the Ras Tanura peninsula; the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted and destroyed four drones over Ras Tanura on Wednesday, with falling debris and no reported injuries there. The refinery's capacity is 550,000 b/d. Separately on Thursday, a projectile struck an oil refinery in the southwestern Jazan region, killing a Nepalese worker and injuring several others; the strike sparked a fire and temporarily suspended some operations (Saudi Ministry of Energy via CNN). OPEC+ meets Sunday, November 29 to discuss 2027 production policy; analysts expect current quotas held through January, and two sources told Reuters the group is likely to maintain output targets for at least Q1 2027. - Why it matters to Sharma: This is no longer a headline premium — a suspended loading programme at the world's largest export complex plus a fatal strike on a second refinery in two days is physical supply risk, and Brent has now risen five straight sessions to ~$96.57. Pump prices have not yet followed (Item 4C — retail lags futures by days), but a Brent print holding in the mid-$90s through the OPEC+ meeting ends the "diesel keeps falling" assumption Sharma has enjoyed for two weeks. Watch Sunday's OPEC+ outcome and Monday's AAA print together.
