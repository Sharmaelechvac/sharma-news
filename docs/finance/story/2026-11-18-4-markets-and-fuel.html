Item 4A — Stocks fall a fourth straight day; S&P 500 lowest close since late July; VIX jumps ~11% — Tuesday, November 17 close - Source / links: The Edge US Market Wrap 17 November, Nov 17, 2026 — https://edgeconsultancykw.com/us-market-wrap-17-november-2026/ ; AP figures via TickerGrove, Nov 17, 2026 — https://tickergrove.com/stories/us-stocks-tuesday-close-fourth-straight-fall-nvidia-earnings-20261117 - Key figures: S&P 500 7,340.53 (−76.06, −1.03%) — fourth straight decline and its lowest close since late July; Nasdaq Composite 24,328.38 (−181.38, −0.74%), deeper in correction territory; Dow Jones Industrial Average 50,759.42 (−848.75, −1.64%), the day's weakest major; Nasdaq 100 27,001.78 (−0.72%); Russell 2000 2,701.87 (−1.50%). Every S&P 500 sector fell, financials the most; JPMorgan Chase closed −2.44%. The selloff was led by software stocks and AI names ahead of Nvidia's after-close earnings. The VIX jumped about 11% to ~25.5 (see Could not verify for the conflicting close print). A contributing sentiment factor, reported Tuesday by CNN: the NSA's classified internal network (NSANet) went down — six sources — with the agency citing "temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance" and DNI Jay Clayton attributing it to a software patch for PHP vulnerabilities discovered last month; no evidence of a cyberattack, per CNN and the agency (Item 4A note: https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/17/politics/highly-classified-national-security-communications-network-goes-down?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta). - Why it matters to Sharma: a fourth straight down day with the S&P at a three-and-a-half-month low is the demand-fear side of the same coin as copper's slide and the NAHB print — it does not change Sharma's order book today, but sustained equity weakness is how builder sentiment and buyer confidence crack next. Wednesday's FOMC minutes are the next test.

Q4 revenue guidance: $130 billion, ±3% (consensus ~$125 billion; the quarter includes an extra fiscal week), with zero Data Center compute revenue from China assumed — $2.4 billion of licensed Hopper sales to China in Q3 were halted — and gross margin guided down to 71%–72%. Management raised the fiscal 2028 revenue-growth outlook to ~75% from ~70%, and the Vera Rubin platform generated nearly $20 billion in its first quarter of production shipments. Interim CEO Colette Kress (CFO, serving since Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on November 12) told the call Nvidia will be "disciplined and selective" in financing neocloud customers. In the regular session Tuesday, Nvidia shares fell 0.44% to $155.46 (from Monday's $156.14); after the results, shares gained around 4–5% in after-hours trading per FXStreet/Saxo. - Why it matters to Sharma: the AI-infrastructure cycle's fundamentals held — demand is still outrunning supply — which undercuts the demand-fear story behind copper's slide, but the market's restrained after-hours reaction says investors now discount AI leadership risk. Watch Wednesday's open for whether the beat steadies the broader market and, with it, builder sentiment.

Both remain sharply lower in November (Brent down ~20% on the month). The move followed reports of vessel attacks in and around the Strait of Hormuz and, per a Reuters-sourced evening brief, a U.S. strike on an Iranian missile launch site on Qeshm Island — while the IEA's ~2 million bpd Q4 surplus call kept the rally small. API (Tuesday evening, week ended November 13): U.S. crude stocks +5.28 million barrels, gasoline stocks +5.12 million barrels, distillate inventories +351,000 barrels — the EIA's official report follows Wednesday at 10:30 AM ET. At the pump, AAA's national average on Tuesday was regular gasoline $3.78/gal (down 20 cents on the week) and diesel $5.98/gal (down 17 cents on the week; diesel fell below $6 on Monday, November 16, for the first time since early September, after a late-September peak above $6.50). - Why it matters to Sharma: crude bounced on war headlines, but the pump is still moving the other way — diesel at $5.98 is real relief against the late-September peak, and it undercuts supplier fuel surcharges priced off the spike. Expect pump relief to keep lagging the futures move; check surcharges against the falling weekly trend, not the spike.
