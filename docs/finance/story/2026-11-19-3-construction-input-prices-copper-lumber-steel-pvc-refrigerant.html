Item 3A — Copper plunges to its lowest since April: COMEX December settles −5.30% at $5.6685/lb; intraday low $5.5835 (−6.7%) — Wednesday, November 18 - Source / links: The Edge Commodities Wrap 18 November (exchange settlements), Nov 18, 2026 — https://edgeconsultancykw.com/commodities-wrap-18-november-2026/ ; Mining.com with Reuters and Bloomberg, Nov 18, 2026, via Kilambi News — https://news.kilambi.com/stories/2026-11-18-copper-plunges-lowest-since-april-miners-rout/ - Published: Wednesday, November 18, 2026 (inside the window). - Key figures: COMEX copper (December) settled at $5.6685/lb, −5.30% on the day — the weakest metal in Wednesday's wrap and the fifth straight down session. Intraday, December copper fell as much as 6.7% to $5.5835/lb ($12,309/t), its lowest since early April, on volume of more than 82,000 lots by midday. The settlement is now about 19% below the $6.8885/lb record settlement of September 9. Drivers cited across the reports: speculative longs unwinding the AI data-centre and tariff trades, a dollar at a 19-month high, and doubts over data-centre demand. The physical market told the opposite story: the Yangshan copper premium jumped to a record ~$350/t (one account says $360/t), Shanghai (SHFE) stocks fell to 27,642 tonnes — the lowest since December 2023 — while roughly 730,000 tonnes sit stockpiled on COMEX (COMEX warehouse stocks were reported at 805,507 short tons). The rout wiped about $49 billion off the biggest copper producers' market value in the day, and about $186 billion in a month (Mining.com). - Why it matters to Sharma: this is the biggest one-day copper drop of the year — wire and cable input costs just fell ~5% in a session, to the cheapest since April. It strengthens Sharma's hand against supplier quotes still priced off September–October levels, but a speculative washout this violent can snap back just as fast; the record Yangshan premium says physical metal is tight even as paper prices fall. Keep quote-validity windows short in both directions.

The LME official cash price for November 18 was $12,750/t, down from Tuesday's official cash of $13,226/t. The rest of the LME complex fell with it: aluminium −2.4% to $2,864/t, zinc −2.9% to $3,567.50/t, nickel −2.9% to $15,985/t, lead −0.2% to $1,868.50/t. Shanghai's most-traded copper contract fell 3.4% to 97,920 yuan/t. - Why it matters to Sharma: both benchmarks confirm the move — this is a global repricing, not a COMEX quirk. LME cash at $12,750/t is the reference embedded in supplier contracts; expect CES and World Electric wire pricing to follow with a lag, and push for the lag to be short on the way down, as it is on the way up.
