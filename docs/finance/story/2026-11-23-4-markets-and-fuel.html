Readings strengthened through the evening: Reuters' Sunday-evening update put S&P 500 futures approximately 2% higher and Nasdaq-100 futures nearly 3% higher (via Qwalisoft), and at 9:32 PM ET Dow futures were +1.54%, S&P 500 futures +1.96%, and Nasdaq-100 futures +2.33% (Stocktwits). The context is Friday's close: S&P 500 6,910.39 (−3.70% on the day, −8.6% on the week, 11.6% below its October 6 record — a correction), Dow 48,147.33 (−1.93%, week −8.0%), Nasdaq Composite 22,617.73 (−5.28%, week −10.0%, its largest weekly decline since March 2020). All three indexes notched their largest one-week point declines on record (Dow Jones Market Data via Barron's). The drivers credited for the bounce: Oracle's statement after Friday's close that it "has secured all the funding we need to fulfill our contract with OpenAI" — tied to a final capital raise of about $20 billion (roughly $10 billion of equity at $116/share and $10 billion of notes, closed November 4) for the five-year, roughly $300 billion OpenAI computing contract — and stabilization measures announced by South Korean, Chinese, and Taiwanese authorities after Asia sold off on Friday's U.S. rout. Sunday futures trade thin; a roughly 2% bounce recovers about a fifth of the week's loss and is not confirmation of a bottom. - Why it matters to Sharma: the futures bounce is the first market signal since Friday that is not a decline, and it matters to Sharma through two channels: builder and supplier share prices (and the credit mood behind them) go into Monday steadier than Friday's close implied, and the Treasury panic bid that pulled mortgage quotes down (Item 2A) may partially unwind if the bounce holds — which would push quoted mortgage money back up. Both channels are decided by Monday's cash session and Wednesday's data, not by Sunday-night futures. Treat the bounce as a weather report, not a forecast.

Item 4B — Oil falls on the Sunday reopen despite the Hormuz strikes: WTI about $82.84, Brent about $91.85 — Sunday evening, November 22 - Source / links: Qwalisoft Market Insights (WSJ front-month prices; Reuters Friday settlements), Week Ahead — Nov 23–27, 2026, verified Sunday evening, Nov 22, 2026 — https://www.qwalisoft.com/Home/MarketInsight/week-ahead-2026-11-23 ; The Wall Street Journal, Oil Remains in Tight Range Amid Surplus Expectations, Gulf Attacks, Nov 2026 — https://www.wsj.com/finance/commodities-futures/oil-falls-amid-risk-off-sentiment-c478ebdc ; The Energy Brief, Sun futures: tanker incident at the blockade line, Nov 22, 2026 — https://theenergybrief94.substack.com/p/sun-futures-tanker-incident-at-the - Published: Sunday evening, November 22, 2026 (inside the window). - Key figures: Sunday-evening front-month prices were WTI about $82.84/bbl (−0.7%) and Brent about $91.85/bbl (−0.8%), against Friday settlements of WTI (December) $83.98 and Brent $92.57 — both benchmarks had risen on Friday's session (WTI +0.9%, Brent +0.6%) while posting a third straight weekly loss (Brent nearly −4% on the week; expiring December WTI about −5%). In other words, the market's first chance to price Saturday's two tanker strikes (prior edition) and Saturday evening's Bahrain facility attack (Item 4C) produced a decline, not a premium. The flow data explain the shrug and the risk at once: transits through the Strait of Hormuz remained 42% below prewar levels in the seven days to November 21 (Kpler preliminary data, via WSJ), and inflows to the UAE's Fujairah port — a proxy for oil exiting the strait — are 67% lower than their October peak (QuantCube Technology, via WSJ). Snapshot variance in thin Sunday trading is real: The Energy Brief's Sunday print showed WTI $83.30 (−0.1%) and Brent $92.17 (−0.4%) — see Could not verify. The next OPEC+ quota decision is due November 29. - Why it matters to Sharma: the surplus story is still beating the war story at the futures board — good for Sharma's fuel and delivery costs this week, and consistent with the pump trend in Item 4D. But the flow numbers say the strait is moving roughly three-fifths of its normal volume while tankers stage at Kharg for a possible blockade run (Item 4C): the price is calm precisely because the confrontation has not yet interrupted a cargo. A confirmed interruption is the one event that reprices diesel faster than any Fed decision — keep fuel-cost validity windows short on anything priced this week.

Item 4C — UPDATE: Hormuz — Bahrain reports two injured in Saturday's facility attack and seeks UN action Sunday; 14+ tankers staged at Kharg, no departure confirmed — Saturday, November 21 – Sunday, November 22 - Source / links: Central Oklahoma News (AFP / Al Jazeera / Dawn reporting), Bahrain says Iranian attack set industrial site ablaze as UKMTO reports tanker hits near Strait of Hormuz, Nov 22, 2026 — https://centraloklahomanews.com/bahrain-iran-attack-industrial-site-tankers-hit-strait-of-hormuz/ ; The Library Media, Global Commodities — Market Opening: Hormuz Standoff and Kharg Tanker Buildup Frame the Sunday Reopen (Nov 22, 2026), Nov 22, 2026 — https://thelibraryestate.com/global-commodities-market-opening-hormuz-standoff-and-kharg-tanker-buildup-frame-the-sunday-reopen-nov-22-2026/ ; Military-Affairs.com, Iran strikes US bases in Bahrain: fire at an industrial facility as Tehran readies a convoy of 14 tankers at Kharg to break the blockade, updated Nov 22, 2026 — https://military-affairs.com/en/wire/iran/2026/11/21/iran-attacks-bahrain-us-bases-kharg-tanker-convoy ; The Energy Brief, Sun futures: tanker incident at the blockade line, Nov 22, 2026 — https://theenergybrief94.substack.com/p/sun-futures-tanker-incident-at-the - Published: the attack occurred Saturday evening, November 21; the injury update, the UN call, and the Kharg reporting were published Sunday, November 22 (inside the window). - Key figures: Bahrain's Interior Ministry said Sunday that Saturday's attack injured two people — a Bahraini citizen, in stable condition, and a Pakistani resident, in critical condition — updating its Saturday statement, which reported a fire and material damage at a civilian industrial facility with no injuries. Bahrain has not officially identified the facility; residents reported smoke over Sitra Island, home to industrial sites including the Bapco refinery (AFP). Bahrain's Defence Force said air defences intercepted several Iranian aerial attacks late Saturday, and on Sunday Bahrain's Foreign Ministry called for UN Security Council action and compensation from Iran, rejecting the IRGC's justifications and saying the targeted facility was exclusively civilian and industrial. The IRGC said it launched missiles and drones at U.S. military positions at Al-Riffa and Awali and claimed it struck two U.S. Patriot radars; Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said reopening discussions were pointless until Tehran's demands were met. Separately, more than 14 Iranian-flagged tankers, many of them very large crude carriers, were reported gathered at or near Kharg Island, which handles roughly 85–90% of Iran's crude exports, ahead of an expected convoy challenge to the U.S. naval blockade in the coming days, with China named as the intended buyer; Kpler estimated about 43 million barrels of Iranian oil stranded inside the blockade zone. No official has confirmed a sailing order and no departure was confirmed on Sunday. UKMTO also logged a further Sunday incident — a tanker meeting military forces near the blockade line in the Gulf of Oman — with no vessel or force named and no confirmation it involved a Kharg tanker (The Energy Brief). UKMTO counts 31 vessels targeted in and around Hormuz from September 30 to November 9 and about 19 more in the first 18 days of November, the highest rate since the war began. The two Saturday tanker strikes near Khasab (prior edition) still carry no reported casualties. - Why it matters to Sharma: this is the escalation ladder, rung by rung: daily tanker hits, a struck industrial facility onshore with casualties now confirmed, a UN referral, and a staged 14-tanker convoy whose sailing would be the blockade's first real test. None of it has yet stopped a cargo, which is why oil fell Sunday (Item 4B) — but the Kharg convoy is a dated, visible trigger: if it sails and is turned back or hit, the surplus pricing that has carried diesel down for seven straight days is the first thing that goes. This remains the single largest near-term risk to Sharma's fuel, delivery, and material-freight costs.
