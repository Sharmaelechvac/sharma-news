Numbers first. Sunday, November 22, 2026 was a closed-market day for U.S. cash markets — no stock, bond, or cash commodity session — but futures reopened at 6:00 PM ET Sunday and produced this edition's two hard market prints. (1) Equity futures bounced after the worst week since 2020: just after the 6:00 PM ET reopen, S&P 500 futures were up 1.3%, Nasdaq-100 futures up 2.0%, and Dow futures up 0.8% (nearly 400 points) (Barron's); later Sunday-evening readings were higher — S&P 500 futures approximately +2% and Nasdaq-100 futures nearly +3% (Reuters via Qwalisoft), and at 9:32 PM ET Dow futures were +1.54%, S&P 500 futures +1.96%, Nasdaq-100 futures +2.33% (Stocktwits). The bounce followed Friday's closes — S&P 500 6,910.39 (−3.70%, week −8.6%), Dow 48,147.33 (−1.93%, week −8.0%), Nasdaq Composite 22,617.73 (−5.28%, week −10.0%) — and was credited to Oracle's post-close statement that its OpenAI contract funding is secured and to stabilization measures by South Korean, Chinese, and Taiwanese authorities. Futures are not Monday's open. (2) Oil fell on the reopen — it did not spike: Sunday-evening front-month WTI was about $82.84/bbl (−0.7%) and Brent about $91.85/bbl (−0.8%), against Friday settlements of WTI (December) $83.98 and Brent $92.57 (WSJ via Qwalisoft). Two days of tanker strikes at Hormuz bought no premium: Kpler puts Hormuz transits still 42% below prewar levels in the seven days to November 21, yet the market is pricing the oil as still flowing. (3) The Fed argument escalated on Sunday television: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CBS's Face the Nation that "many people believe that they may be making a policy mistake if they over-tighten," citing "a substantial tightening in financial conditions over the past few months" and blaming part of the selloff on "very hawkish remarks from the Federal Reserve" — he named no official and stated no preferred December outcome. Pershing Square's Bill Ackman posted the same day that the Fed is about to make "another mistake" on December 9, that financial conditions have tightened "massively," and that the Fed should consider a 3% inflation target instead of hiking toward 2%. Sunday-evening fed-funds futures implied 88.6% for a 25 bp December hike and 11.4% for no change, with a 50 bp hike no longer priced (Reuters via Qwalisoft) — down from Friday's more-than-98% CME read in the prior edition, a move in pricing, not in policy. The policy rate stands at 3.75%–4.00%; the FOMC meets December 8–9, the communications blackout begins at midnight Saturday, November 28, and New York Fed President Williams speaks Monday at 4:00 PM ET. (4) Pump prices fell a seventh straight day: AAA's Sunday national averages were regular $3.645/gal and diesel $5.845/gal (Saturday: $3.6594 / $5.8649) — diesel under $6 for a seventh straight day. Florida AAA Sunday: regular about $3.47, diesel about $5.59 (Saturday: $3.4771 / $5.6049). (5) Mortgage quotes did not move on Sunday: Zillow's Sunday purchase table is identical to Saturday's — 30-year fixed 7.18%, 15-year 6.57%, 5/1 ARM 7.11%, 30-year VA 6.66% — and the source itself says the Sunday table "matches Saturday's figures, as is common on a weekend, so this is not a new daily change." The 30-year refinance average was reported at 7.44% (headline: down 6 bp). MND publishes no Sunday print (Friday: 7.33%); Freddie Mac's weekly is 7.28%. (6) Hormuz update: Bahrain's Interior Ministry said Sunday that two people were injured in Saturday's attack on a civilian industrial facility — a Bahraini citizen in stable condition and a Pakistani resident in critical condition — revising Saturday's initial no-injury report for that facility (the two tanker incidents still carry no reported casualties). Bahrain's Foreign Ministry on Sunday called for UN Security Council action and compensation from Iran; the IRGC said it targeted U.S. Patriot positions at Al-Riffa and Awali. More than 14 Iranian-flagged tankers were reported staged at Kharg Island (85–90% of Iran's crude exports) for a possible convoy challenge to the U.S. blockade, with about 43 million barrels of Iranian oil estimated stranded inside the blockade zone (Kpler) — no departure was confirmed on Sunday. (7) Credit: President Trump said Saturday on Fox News that he would work with the incoming Democratic Congress to cap credit card interest rates at 10% (AP, published Sunday) — against a median card rate of about 22.2% (New York Fed August survey) and a Federal Reserve Q3 average of 22.36% on interest-assessed accounts; card balances are at a record $1.29 trillion. No bill has been introduced in the incoming Congress; the existing Sanders–Hawley bill (S. 381, 10% for five years) has sat in committee since February 2025, and Democrats do not take control until January 2027. (8) Central Florida price cuts eased: 1,148 listings cut prices in the week ending November 21 (pull dated November 22), down 90 from the prior week's 1,238, at an average cut of 3.34%, with 652 (56.79%) on the market 60+ days (Stellar MLS via EIN Presswire). Lake County was the exception, up 62 to 308 cuts. (9) Bankruptcy: two real-estate LLCs filed Chapter 11 on Sunday — BTC Schenectady, LLC (Subchapter V, N.D.N.Y., case 26-11389) and LP 5301 NH Ave NW LLC (D.D.C., case 26-00614) — and no construction-trade filing dated Sunday was found. There was no new copper, lumber, steel, PVC, or R-454B print (no Sunday trading), no CES or World Electric news, no SBA final rule, no new housing-starts release (next is December 17), and no Florida statistical release (Florida Realtors next: December 16) in the window. Ahead: Wednesday, November 25 is the week's data cliff — October PCE (consensus headline +0.3% m/m, 3.8% y/y; core +0.3%, 3.2% y/y), Q3 GDP second estimate (consensus 3.5%, advance 3.4%), jobless claims, durable goods, new-home sales, EIA inventories, and the Beige Book, all in a Thanksgiving-shortened week (markets closed Thursday, early close Friday); OPEC+ meets November 29.
