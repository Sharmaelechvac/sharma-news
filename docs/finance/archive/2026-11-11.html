Sharma Electric & HVAC — Daily Finance News, Nov 10–11, 2026

Window: Roughly 24 hours before Wed Nov 11, 2026, 4:41 PM ET (Nov 10 ~4:41 PM ET – Nov 11 4:41 PM ET). All dates below were verified on the source pages themselves (datelines, embargo lines, publication stamps, or computed-at times shown on the page). Summary

One story dominated the 24 hours: a hotter-than-expected October CPI on Tuesday Nov 10 killed the four-week slide in mortgage rates, put a December Fed rate hike — possibly 50 bp — on the table, and pushed the 10-year Treasury to ~5%. That is bearish for Florida new-home demand heading into winter. Copper stayed historically high and physically tight. There was no material 24-hour news on HVAC equipment / R-454B / tariffs, on CES or World Electric, on Census housing starts/permits/new-home sales, or on slowdowns, layoffs, defaults or slow pay at DR Horton, Lennar, Pulte, Meritage or Strada Services — those categories are flagged explicitly below.

Top 8 — ranked by importance to Sharma Electric & HVAC

1. October CPI comes in hot: +0.4% headline, +0.3% core for a third straight month

  • Headline: Consumer Price Index — October 2026 (USDL-26-1685)
  • Key numbers: CPI-U +0.4% m/m seasonally adjusted (forecast +0.3%; September +0.6%); core (less food & energy) +0.3% (forecast +0.2%; third straight +0.3%); shelter +0.3%, accounting for over 30% of the monthly increase; food +0.5%; energy +0.7%; electricity +0.9%. No 12-month rate was published — BLS did not collect October 2025 data during the lapse in appropriations.
  • Publication date: Tue Nov 10, 2026, 8:30 AM ET (embargo line on the release)
  • Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm
  • Why it matters to Sharma: This single print reversed the rate relief builders and buyers had just gotten, so expect softer buyer traffic and tougher builder pricing pressure on winter starts.

2. Fed officials open the door to a 50 bp December hike after Waller's CPI trigger is met

  • Headline: Fed officials open door to a quicker round of rate hikes as inflation worsens
  • Key numbers: Current policy rate 3.75%–4.00%; Gov. Christopher Waller said hours before the data he would back +25 bp at the Dec 8–9 meeting unless core CPI hit 0.3% or more — it did — in which case "a 50-basis-point hike… should be considered"; 50 bp odds jumped to ~15% mid-morning, ~10% by late afternoon (from near zero); traders priced three 25 bp hikes by mid-2027 vs two before; Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan said the Fed needs at least +50 bp more in total before pausing. CME FedWatch via MarketWatch put a 25 bp hike above 85%.
  • Publication date: Nov 10, 2026 (Reuters, Washington, Nov 10); updated Nov 11, 2026, 05:19 UTC
  • Source: Reuters via WABX — https://wabx.net/2026/11/10/investors-boost-odds-of-half-point-fed-hike-as-core-cpi-rise-meets-wallers-bar/
  • Why it matters to Sharma: A December hike — especially 50 bp — raises Sharma's own borrowing costs and builders' construction-loan costs at the same time it cools end-buyer demand.

3. Mortgage rates jump 12 bp in one day to 7.30%, ending four weeks of easing

  • Headline: Mortgage rates jump sharply higher, after easing for four straight weeks. Here's why
  • Key numbers: Mortgage News Daily 30-year fixed +12 bp Monday→Tuesday to 7.30%, the largest one-day jump in more than a month. The prior week's MBA data (week ending Nov 6, released Nov 11): applications +6.2%, purchase applications +8% on the week but −18% year over year, refinances +5% on the week but −57% year over year, MBA weekly average rate 7.21% (from 7.30%), Freddie Mac weekly average 7.18%.
  • Publication date: Nov 11, 2026
  • Source: CNBC (Diana Olick), as syndicated — https://review.tokyo-wire.com/2026/11/11/mortgage-rates-jump-sharply-higher-after-easing-for-four-straight-weeks-heres-why/ ; corroborating MBA release coverage: Realtor.com, Nov 11, 2026 — https://www.realtor.com/news/trends/mortgage-applications-rates-mba-survey-november-11-2026/
  • Why it matters to Sharma: At 7.30% daily rates, production builders' entry-level buyers in Florida are payment-capped again, which is the fastest channel from rates to fewer rough-in and trim starts for Sharma.

4. Treasury yields surge: 10-year to 4.99%, 2-year +19 bp in its biggest one-day rise in two years

  • Headline: Why stocks are falling and 10-year yields are back near 5% after Tuesday's inflation report
  • Key numbers: 10-year Treasury +12 bp to 4.99% on Nov 10 (intraday prints above 5.00% reported elsewhere the same day); 2-year +19 bp to 4.75%, on track for its biggest one-day rise in two years (FactSet); S&P 500 −0.2%, Dow −0.7%, Nasdaq −0.9% that day.
  • Publication date: Nov 10, 2026, 11:28 AM ET (timestamp on the page)
  • Source: MarketWatch via Morningstar — https://www.morningstar.com/news/marketwatch/2026111071/why-stocks-are-falling-and-10-year-yields-are-back-near-5-after-tuesdays-inflation-report
  • Why it matters to Sharma: The 10-year at ~5% is what prices mortgages and builder debt, so this move — not the Fed's current rate — is what will show up first in builder incentives, cancellations and start pacing.

5. Copper stays near record levels and physically tight; Comex settles +0.88% at $6.2745/lb

  • Headline: Copper Rebounds as China Supply Tightness Keeps Physical Market in Focus / Comex Copper Settles 0.88% Higher at $6.2745 — Data Talk
  • Key numbers: Comex front-month copper +5.50¢ (+0.88%) to $6.2745/lb on Nov 11; −2.85% month-to-date but +11.45% year-to-date and +23.28% vs a year ago; 52-week/record high $6.8035 (Sep 9, 2026). LME 3-month copper recovered toward $13,900/t; China's Yangshan import premium at record levels (Reuters/Andy Home, Nov 10: record $230/t, LME cash premium $195/t over 3-month, Shanghai exchange stocks 33,848 t — a three-year low — with ~85% of global exchange stocks held in the US on the tariff trade); North American clean-copper scrap benchmarks fell 9¢/lb on Nov 10 after rising 8¢ the prior session.
  • Publication date: Nov 11, 2026 (both ScrapMonster and the Dow Jones/Morningstar settlement report, 2:04 PM ET)
  • Source: ScrapMonster — https://www.scrapmonster.com/news/copper/copper-rebounds-as-china-supply-tightness-keeps-lme-prices-under-pressure-2026-11-11/100452 ; Morningstar / Dow Jones — https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202611116999/comex-copper-settles-088-higher-at-62745-data-talk ; Reuters column via Devdiscourse (published Nov 10, 2026) — https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/international/4004161-roi-fund-sellers-china-buyers-squeeze-coppers-tariff-trade-andy-home
  • Why it matters to Sharma: Wire and copper-bearing material remain priced near all-time highs with tight nearby supply, so quotes should stay short-dated and Sharma should not absorb copper moves on fixed-price builder contracts.

6. Early October existing-home sales fell 13.7% YoY; NAR consensus sits near a new cycle low

  • Headline: October Existing Sales Preview: Early Markets Down 13.7% | NAR Report
  • Key numbers: Closed sales in Calculated Risk's early-reporting local markets −13.7% YoY in October (not seasonally adjusted; September was −5.2%); new listings −4.9% YoY; inventory +4.3% YoY. Consensus for the NAR report due Thu Nov 12: 3.87 million SAAR, down from 3.99 million in September and only 20,000 above the cycle low of 3.85 million (Oct 2023).
  • Publication date: Nov 11, 2026
  • Source: United States Real Estate Investor, reporting Calculated Risk's Nov 11 second look — https://www.unitedstatesrealestateinvestor.com/calculated-risk-october-2026-existing-sales-preview-cycle-low/
  • Why it matters to Sharma: A near-cycle-low resale market means fewer move-up buyers freeing up demand for new builds and more resale competition against builders' Florida inventory homes.

7. Housing inventory keeps building as demand stays down YoY

  • Headline: High Frequency Data: Inventory Up, Demand Down Year-over-year
  • Key numbers: Altos Research active single-family inventory +7.2% YoY for the week through Nov 6 (previous week +6.8%) and +1.1% vs the same week in 2019; Realtor.com inventory +8.8% YoY, its highest weekly growth since late January, driven by listings accumulating rather than a flood of new sellers; MBA purchase activity −18% YoY (same release as item 3).
  • Publication date: Nov 11, 2026, 13:16 UTC
  • Source: Calculated Risk — https://calculatedrisk.substack.com/p/high-frequency-data-inventory-up-2f9
  • Why it matters to Sharma: Rising unsold inventory is what triggers builder incentive spending, spec-home slowdowns and price squeezes on subcontractors — watch Florida spec counts, not just starts.

8. Tampa Bay permits: Lennar and DR Horton still the most active builders — no local pullback in the count

  • Headline: Most active general contractors in Tampa Bay, last 90 days
  • Key numbers: Building permits naming the firm as general contractor over the last 90 days: Lennar Homes LLC 56 (#1), DR Horton Inc 22 (#3); the top 25 contractors account for 354 permits combined. Trade sub-permits are excluded from the count, per the page's method note.
  • Publication date: Updated Nov 11, 2026; computed Nov 11, 2026, 3:05 PM ET (on the page)
  • Source: Groundbreak — https://pro.groundbreak.city/tampa/stats/most-active-contractors
  • Why it matters to Sharma: In Sharma's core Tampa Bay market the two biggest production builders are still pulling permits at the top of the table, so the near-term local pipeline has not cracked — the risk in items 1–4 is forward-looking, not yet in the permit data.
